Consumer Behaviour · MKTG7503 · 2026 · Group Project

AI & Pre-Purchase Decision Making

Why are consumers asking AI before they buy?

This project looks at the growing use of generative AI during pre-purchase information search and decision making. Our group is investigating why consumers are turning to AI, what they use it for, and how factors such as trust, involvement, risk and autonomy affect that behaviour.

My contribution

Alongside the group research, I worked independently on the early research mapping. I built an affinity map to bring together literature and ideas around information search, convenience, information overload, trust, decision confidence, task characteristics and consumer autonomy.

Where it is now

The project is currently in progress. We are developing the research questions, conducting consumer interviews and moving towards theory-informed analysis.

Business Analytics · BSAN7211 · 2026 · Individual Project

Airbnb Data Monetisation Strategy

What else could Airbnb do with the data it already has?

I worked independently on a data monetisation strategy for Airbnb. I started by looking at the different kinds of information generated across the Airbnb ecosystem, including guest behaviour, listings, transactions, supply and the broader travel journey.

What I did

I mapped Airbnb's data ecosystem, looked at how the company already creates value from data, identified potential areas for expansion and developed a way to compare different monetisation opportunities.

The final idea

The portfolio was structured around three directions: Improve, Wrap and Sell. The aim was to move from individual data signals towards connected insights, actions and new forms of business value.

03 / How I work

I don't usually jump straight into the analysis. :)

I like to spend some time getting a feel for the problem first. I'll map things out, look at the data, make a few charts, read around the topic and generally try to understand what I'm looking at before I get too deep into the details.

I like having that bigger picture first. Once I understand the landscape, I can figure out what is actually worth investigating and where I should spend my time.

Sometimes that means a Miro board. Sometimes it is a few messy charts, a spreadsheet, a notebook or a page full of questions.

This is roughly how I get from "what is going on here?" to something I can actually explain.

01

Get the picture

Understand the problem, the context and what I'm actually looking at.

02

Explore

Map things out, look for patterns, read around the topic and ask a lot of questions.

03

Dig deeper

Once something looks interesting, investigate it properly using methods that make sense for the question.

04

Make sense of it

Work out what the results actually tell me, including what they don't tell me.

05

Tell the story

Turn the work into something another person can understand and use.

A glimpse into the messy middle :)

I love mapping things out.

A Miro board, a few charts, a pile of notes or a messy collection of ideas can sometimes tell me more than jumping straight into the final analysis. It helps me see what connects, what doesn't, and where the interesting questions might actually be.
