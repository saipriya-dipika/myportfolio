01 / A bit of context

I started with the technical side. Then I kept getting curious about the questions around it.

My undergraduate background is in Computer Science, and my current degree is in Data Science. Along the way, I've found myself working on things that don't fit neatly into one category: consumer behaviour, marketing, financial markets, AI, business strategy and research.

I don't think that means I need one label for everything I've done. I enjoy learning something properly, working through the problem, and seeing where it takes me.

03 / How I work

I usually start with a question, not a method.

The interesting part of a project is rarely just the final chart or report. I like showing the thinking that happened before it.

01

Ask

What am I actually trying to understand?

02

Explore

Read, map, compare, question assumptions and look for patterns.

03

Analyse

Use the methods that make sense for the question.

04

Make sense of it

Work out what the results actually mean.

05

Communicate

Turn the work into something another person can understand and use.

A glimpse into the messy middle

My Miro boards show some of the thinking before the final answer.

I use mapping, clustering and visual notes to work through literature and ideas before deciding where the actual research question should go.
